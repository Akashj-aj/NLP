About This Project

A web app that reads a customer complaint and automatically figures out what category it belongs to — like a payment issue, an order problem, a refund request, and so on.

1. Dataset Details

The app is trained on the Bitext Customer Support Dataset — a large collection of customer support messages covering common service topics.

  • Total Messages: 26,872 original (24,635 after removing duplicates).
  • Categories: 11 — ACCOUNT, CANCEL, CONTACT, DELIVERY, FEEDBACK, INVOICE, ORDER, PAYMENT, REFUND, SHIPPING, SUBSCRIPTION.
  • Training / Testing: 80% for training (19,878 samples), 20% for testing (4,927 samples).

2. NLP Preprocessing Pipeline

Before the model reads a complaint, the text goes through a few cleaning steps to make it easier to understand:

  1. Remove identifiers: Order IDs, UPI IDs, transaction numbers, emails, and phone numbers are replaced with simple tags (like <order_id>) so they don't confuse the model.
  2. Lowercase & expand contractions: Text is lowercased and words like "can't" are written out as "cannot".
  3. Remove symbols: Punctuation and special characters are removed.
  4. Remove common words: Words like "the", "is", "a" are removed since they don't help with classification. But negation words like not, never, didn't, can't are kept because they change the meaning.
  5. Convert to numbers (TF-IDF): The cleaned text is turned into numbers the model can work with, based on how often words and phrases appear.

3. Machine Learning Algorithms

Three different models are trained and compared. You can choose which one to use when classifying a complaint:

Multinomial Naive Bayes

Uses word frequencies to figure out which category a complaint most likely belongs to. Simple, fast, and works well with text.

Logistic Regression

Learns which words are most important for each category and uses that to make a prediction.

Linear SVM

Finds the clearest boundary between categories. Usually the most accurate for text classification.

All three models are trained on the same data and evaluated separately. You can compare their accuracy on the Model Comparison page.
